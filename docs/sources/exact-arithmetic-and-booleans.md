The error bounds of the implicit points' approximations (`segment_point`, `plane_value`, `planes_approx`) and of
the filters on them are derived in the code comments.

The bit bounds of the fixed-width predicates (`FixedArith.Orient`, `FixedArith.InCircle`) are derived by
`tools/PredicateCompiler` (build-time tool, not shipped), own code: magnitude bounds over exact
integers. The idea of deriving the bounds of a predicate from its formula by machine follows A. Meyer, S. Pion,
*FPG: A code generator for fast and certified geometric predicates*, RNC 2008 (idea only, no code read).

The exact stage of `ExactPredicates.Orient2d`, `Orient3d` and `InCircle` beyond the range of the expansions
(`math/Generated/DoublePredicates.cs`, generated by the monomial pass of `tools/PredicateCompiler`, own code;
docs/design/EXACT_FALLBACK_MONOMIALS.md) adds the exact monomials of the determinant, each a product of mantissas shifted
to its exponent, into two fixed-point accumulators and compares them. The accumulator is the long accumulator of
U. Kulisch, V. Snyder, *The exact dot product as basic tool for long interval arithmetic*, Computing 91, 2011, extended
to products of two to four factors (the limb counts are derived by the pass); the reduction of a determinant to the
exact sign of a sum of products follows H. Ratschek, J. Rokne, *Exact computation of the sign of a finite sum*, Applied
Mathematics and Computation 1999, and M. Gavrilova, H. Ratschek, J. Rokne, *Exact computation of Delaunay and power
triangulations*, Reliable Computing 6, 2000; generated straight-line integer code per predicate follows S. Fortune,
C. Van Wyk, *Static analysis yields efficient exact integer arithmetic for computational geometry*, ACM TOG 15, 1996;
W. F. Mascarenhas, *Computing the exact sign of sums of products with floating point arithmetic*, arXiv:2109.07838,
2021, treats the same problem with scaled doubles (the methods from the papers; no code read).

The generated indirect predicates (`math/Generated/IndirectPredicates.cs`, orient3d of line-plane and three-plane
points without constructing them) follow M. Attene, *Indirect Predicates for Geometric Constructions*,
Computer-Aided Design 2020, and G. Cherchi, M. Livesu, R. Scateni, M. Attene, *Fast and Robust Mesh Arrangements
using Floating-point Arithmetic*, ACM TOG 2020 (the method from the papers; no code read). Generated by
`tools/PredicateCompiler`, own code.

The polyhedral envelope (`docs/design/ENVELOPE.md`; `mesh_ops/PolyhedralEnvelope.cs`, internal, own code) follows B. Wang, T. Schneider, Y. Hu,
M. Attene, D. Panozzo, *Exact and Efficient Polyhedral Envelope Containment Check*, ACM TOG 39(4), 2020; its reference
code `fast-envelope` (MIT) serves as a reference only; Geogram and the predicate libraries bundled with it were not
read for it. Since 2026-11-03 geogram's own BSD-3 code may be read and ported under the rules in [roadmap/sources-surveys.md](../roadmap/sources-surveys.md#geogram-as-a-source-owner-2026-11-03) ("Geogram as a source").

### Caller-first scheduling of the `gParallel` loops (#1502)

Own implementation; no code read. `ForRange`, `ForEach` and the handovers of the Small loops queue helpers to the thread pool
and run the items on the calling thread at once, taking chunks from one shared counter (the dynamic schedule of OpenMP, with
the caller as a participant); the caller waits only for chunks that were taken, not for helpers that never started. Why:
`Parallel.For` waits for every task it queued, and from a thread outside the pool those tasks sit in the global queue behind
the pool's other work ([dotnet/runtime#4727](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/issues/4727): `Parallel` is "at the mercy of the
underlying scheduler" for its tasks; [dotnet/runtime#72981](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/issues/72981); Criteo,
[.NET ThreadPool starvation, and how queuing makes it worse](https://medium.com/criteo-labs/net-threadpool-starvation-and-how-queuing-makes-it-worse-512c8d570527)).
Numbers: `benchmarks/ParallelTinyLoops/README.md`.

`x = 0.082987235660192948` is reached in the tests (the others are special cases of the original that this port's path decides without them); the
`err = 86` margin of the `Tan` accurate path is not distinguished by any test argument. Both are as in the source and not re-derived.
