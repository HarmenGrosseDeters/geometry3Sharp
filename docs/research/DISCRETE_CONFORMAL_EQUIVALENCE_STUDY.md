# Discrete conformal equivalence on the P1 annulus reproducers

Study for priority 1 of [PR #1424](https://github.com/HarmenGrosseDeters/geometry3Sharp/pull/1424). The P1 failure and its existing alternatives are tracked in [PR #1285](https://github.com/HarmenGrosseDeters/geometry3Sharp/pull/1285) and [GEOGRAM_MEASUREMENTS_2026-11.md](GEOGRAM_MEASUREMENTS_2026-11.md).

**Answer to the falsifiable question: partly.**
A full C# prototype of discrete conformal equivalence with Ptolemy intrinsic Delaunay flips ([`benchmarks/DiscreteConformalAnnulusProbe/DiscreteConformalSolver.cs`](../../benchmarks/DiscreteConformalAnnulusProbe/DiscreteConformalSolver.cs)) converges on **17 of 17** procedural P1, jittered, pinched, twisted, bent, conical and two-hole cases in 3–6 damped Newton iterations (about 1–45 ms) using `SparseCholesky`. On every rotationally symmetric annulus (including every P1 cylinder where LSCM fails, the pinched waist and the twisted cylinder) and every disk/cone, the flat intrinsic triangulation unfolds seamlessly into a planar chart (`transHol < 5e-13`, `0` boundary contacts), and the per-vertex UV of that chart evaluated on the **input** triangles, measured like LSCM (`ParameterizationDistortion`), has **0 flipped and 0 degenerate** triangles, also on the cases with intrinsic flips (twisted cylinder, cones). On **asymmetric, independently jittered, bent, or multi-hole annuli**, both tested boundary-curvature prescriptions (uniform per-vertex turning and turning by input arc length) leave a nonzero 2D translational holonomy (`1.1e-4` to `6.6e-2` of diameter), so a seamless uncut planar chart there still needs an explicit hole-closure (holonomy) constraint or a seam cut.

## Method and building blocks

The prototype implements the convex discrete conformal energy of Springborn, Schröder and Pinkall (2008), the hyperbolic Delaunay criterion and cross-ratio-invariant Ptolemy flip update of Bobenko, Pinkall and Springborn (2015), and the intrinsic flip-and-Newton loop of Gillespie, Springborn and Crane (2021):

- **State:** intrinsic halfedge mesh with unscaled log-lengths $\lambda_{ij}$ and vertex scale factors $u_i$. Scaled lengths are $\tilde\ell_{ij} = \exp(\lambda_{ij} + \frac{1}{2}(u_i + u_j))$. Because the scale factors cancel identically in the Ptolemy update, flipping an edge $(i,j)$ to $(k,l)$ updates the unscaled log-length via $\lambda_{kl} = \operatorname{logaddexp}(\lambda_{ik}+\lambda_{jl}, \lambda_{kj}+\lambda_{li}) - \lambda_{ij}$.
- **Flip predicate:** Penner / Bobenko–Pinkall–Springborn hyperbolic Delaunay value
  $$D_{\mathrm{hyp}}(h) = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - e^2}{ab} + \frac{c^2 + d^2 - e^2}{cd} \ge 0,$$
  which needs no square roots and stays well-defined even when an intermediate Newton step temporarily violates Euclidean triangle inequalities.
- **Energy:** the full per-triangle term $2f(\tilde\lambda) - \frac{\pi}{2}\sum\tilde\lambda$ (with $\tilde\lambda = 2\log\tilde\ell$, $f = \frac12\sum\alpha\tilde\lambda + \sum Л(\alpha)$, Lobachevsky function by 8-point Gauss–Legendre) plus $\sum_i \Theta_i u_i$. The $u$-independent part of $-\frac{\pi}{2}\sum\tilde\lambda$ is kept, since it changes under a Ptolemy flip; with it the energies before and after a flip are values of one function of $u$.
- **Newton step:** exact angle gradient $\nabla_i E = \Theta_i^{\mathrm{target}} - \Theta_i(u)$ and the exact Hessian, the cotangent Laplacian $H = \frac{1}{2} L_{\mathrm{cot}}$ on the free vertices, solved with `geometry3Sharp`'s own `SparseCholesky`. Weights are not clamped: $L_{\mathrm{cot}}$ of a Euclidean triangulation is positive semi-definite even with negative weights. Over all 26 runs, 2007 interior weights were negative only at round-off (`>= -1.5e-14`, cocircular ties of the symmetric meshes) and 28 boundary weights were negative (down to `-0.169`; the Delaunay flips do not constrain boundary edges).
- **Line search:** backtracking (halving, at most 30 trials) with hyperbolic Delaunay flips on each trial point and the Armijo test $E(u+\alpha d) \le E(u) + 10^{-4}\alpha\nabla E\cdot d$ on the full energy. Near convergence the decrease (about the squared residual) falls below the round-off of $E$; then a trial is accepted if $E$ does not rise by more than its round-off bound and the max angle residual decreases (the `fallback` count in the table; 7 of 111 accepted steps).
- **Boundary modes tested:** `turn` (uniform per-vertex loop turning, $+2\pi/N_{\mathrm{out}}$ on the outer loop and $-2\pi/N_{\mathrm{in}}$ on each inner hole loop, one vertex pinned), `turn-len` (the same total turning per loop, distributed by input arc length: vertex share $(\ell_{\mathrm{prev}}+\ell_{\mathrm{next}})/(2P)$) and `u_b=0` (fixed boundary scale factors $u_b = 0$, free boundary curvature).
- **Layout & holonomy check:** BFS spanning-tree unfolding of the final intrinsic Delaunay triangulation, checking rotational and translational mismatch across every non-tree interior edge and exact segment intersection (`ExactPredicates.Orient2d`) on all nonadjacent boundary edges when seamless. The layout places every third vertex to the left of its edge, so the laid-out intrinsic triangles are positively oriented **by construction**; the probe only checks them for (scale-relative) degeneracy and edge-length match. The measured injectivity check is on the input triangles (column `Input UV`).

The last is a consistency check, not a finding: for a Euclidean diamond $(a^2+b^2-e^2)/(ab) = 2\cos\gamma$, and $\cos\gamma_1 + \cos\gamma_2 \ge 0 \iff \gamma_1+\gamma_2 \le \pi \iff \cot\gamma_1 + \cot\gamma_2 \ge 0$, so the two signs agree by a theorem; non-Euclidean diamonds are skipped.

### Key findings from the solver run

1. **Every P1 case where LSCM fails is flattened seamlessly and injectively.** On the four symmetric P1 cylinders, the pinched waist and the twisted cylinder (6 cases where LSCM fails and `SpectralConformalParameterization` refuses multi-boundary topology), CETM + Ptolemy converges in 3–4 Newton iterations and unfolds seamlessly with `0` boundary contacts and `0+0` flipped/degenerate input triangles. Five of the six need no intrinsic flip at all; the twisted cylinder needs 192 initial flips (its input triangulation is not Delaunay), and its input-triangle UV is still flip-free.
2. **Non-Euclidean intermediate states happen, but rarely.** In 8 of the 111 accepted Newton iterates (on 4 of the 26 runs), the trial scale factors made 34 triangles of the current triangulation violate the triangle inequality before the hyperbolic Delaunay Ptolemy flips restored a Euclidean triangulation. So the existing Euclidean `IntrinsicTriangulation.FlipEdge` cannot be used directly inside the Newton loop: the Ptolemy update and hyperbolic flip test are needed, even if only in a few steps.
3. **Curvature-only prescription with the tested modes does NOT close asymmetric holes.** With uniform per-vertex turning, the independently jittered cylinders leave `1.6e-4` to `5.3e-3` relative gap, the bent tube `3.05%` and the two-hole plate `4.04%`; with turning by input arc length, `1.1e-4` to `5.2e-3`, `3.05%` and `6.55%`. In both modes the rotational holonomy vanishes to `3e-15..7e-14 rad` (seam edges are parallel: the total turning of every loop is $\pm 2\pi$), yet the 2D translational period around each hole is nonzero. With fixed boundary scale `u_b = 0` on the bent tube, rotational holonomy is also nonzero (`0.221 rad`). The measured claim covers these two turning distributions only. The expectation that it holds for any fixed rule is a dimension count, not a measurement: the translational period of each hole is two real numbers, which a curvature rule chosen without regard to them sets to zero only by symmetry. A general uncut annulus solver therefore needs either a seam cut (which the UV atlas already does) or an explicit holonomy-closing constraint (two per hole) on the boundary curvature or cones.

## Fit with `IntrinsicTriangulation` and exact arithmetic

- **Connectivity & correspondence:** `IntrinsicTriangulation` already has halfedge flips, signposts, and integer normal coordinates (`IntrinsicIntegerCoordinates`, `IntrinsicCommonSubdivision`, [PR #1311](https://github.com/HarmenGrosseDeters/geometry3Sharp/pull/1311)). Normal coordinates depend only on connectivity, so they should carry over to Ptolemy flips; the probe does not exercise this (it keeps its own halfedge arrays and no correspondence), so the reuse is an expectation, not a measured result. What is missing is a separate Ptolemy-length / hyperbolic-flip path (since `FlipEdge` unfolds Euclidean triangles and enforces convexity preconditions that break during intermediate Newton steps) plus post-solve projective/barycentric interpolation onto the common subdivision.
- **Exact vs. double flip predicates:** Part 2 of the probe compares `ExactPredicates.InCircle`, an exact `BigInteger` evaluation of the degree-4 Penner hyperbolic polynomial $(a^2+b^2-e^2)cd + (c^2+d^2-e^2)ab$ on binary64 lengths, its `double` evaluation, `double` cotangent sum, and `IntrinsicTriangulation.IsDelaunay`:

- **What can and cannot be exact:**
  - The hyperbolic Delaunay sign $(a^2+b^2-e^2)cd + (c^2+d^2-e^2)ab$ is an exact degree-4 polynomial for exact rational/dyadic edge lengths, and the Ptolemy update $(ac+bd)/e$ is rational (leaving the dyadic ring after one division).
  - However, converting 3D coordinates to edge lengths (`sqrt`) and scaling by $\exp((u_i+u_j)/2)$ are transcendental. As the `±2^-54` rows show, once `sqrt(2)` rounds to `double`, **even exact `BigInteger` arithmetic on the stored lengths cannot recover the coordinate-level `InCircle` sign** — both signs collapse to `-1`. At `±2^-40`, `HypPoly` separates the signs cleanly while `IntrinsicTriangulation.IsDelaunay` still accepts both due to its `1e-12` tolerance. During all 26 solver runs, `double` `HypPoly` and `cotSum` never disagreed in sign on a Euclidean diamond (`0` disagreements), as the theorem above says they must.

## Recommendation

**Park as a P1 annulus fix; keep as a candidate only if a caller specifically needs disk/cut-chart conformal flattening of an intrinsic Delaunay triangulation.**

- **Why park for P1 (#1285):** P1 asks for uncut annular / multi-hole UV charts. With both tested boundary-curvature prescriptions, curvature-prescribed discrete conformal equivalence leaves nonzero translational holonomy on every asymmetric or jittered annulus (`1.1e-4` to `6.6e-2` of diameter), and a fixed rule is not expected to close it (finding 3). Closing holes requires an extra holonomy constraint (plus projective common-subdivision interpolation if flips occurred), making it substantially heavier than virtual-fan hole closure or seam cutting (`#1285`) for a P1 fix.
- **Expected build cost if ever resumed:** ~1–2 Tier-L PRs (Ptolemy-length mode + integer normal-coordinate tracking on `IntrinsicTriangulation`, damped Newton driver on `SparseCholesky`, projective interpolation onto `IntrinsicCommonSubdivision`, plus holonomy closing for multi-boundary charts).
- **Guarantee:** the flat metric of a converged solve is a Euclidean intrinsic triangulation with positive-area triangles, so its layout is locally injective by construction (the layout code does not measure this). What the probe measures is the input-mesh map: on the 11 seamless runs (rotationally symmetric annuli and the disks/cones) the per-vertex UV has `0` flipped and `0` degenerate input triangles and `0` boundary contacts. This is not a guarantee for input triangles in general: after intrinsic flips the input edges are not straight in the flat metric, and a per-vertex linear UV can fold. Global injectivity on general curved charts still requires an explicit `UVOverlap` check.
