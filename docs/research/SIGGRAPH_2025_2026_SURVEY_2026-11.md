## 2. Deep Dive: *Fast Sparse Matrix Permutation for Mesh-Based Direct Solvers* (Zarebavani et al., SIGGRAPH 2026)

- **Citation:** B. Zarebavani, A. H. Mahmoud, A. Dodik, C. Yuan, S. D. Porumbescu, J. D. Owens, M. Mehri Dehnavi, J. Solomon, *Fast Sparse Matrix Permutation for Mesh-Based Direct Solvers*, SIGGRAPH Conference Papers '26, DOI `10.1145/3799902.3811189`, arXiv:2602.00898v2 (CC BY 4.0).
- **Code status:** `github.com/BehroozZare/fast-permute` (*Homa*, C++/CUDA/Python; depends on METIS/cuDSS/MKL/CHOLMOD). Its `LICENSE` file was not fetched in this review, so treat as **paper-only**.

### 2.1 Algorithm summary

1. **Patching:** Partition the mesh/graph into connected patches of ~256 vertices (GPU Lloyd's algorithm from RXMesh in the paper; CPU greedy patching also exists in Homa).
2. **Lift to matrix graph (`gmap`):** Map each block of unknowns (e.g., 2×2 Hessian blocks) to its patch. Note: `CholeskyAnalysis.order()` already compresses `BlockSize` unknowns into one block-graph node.
3. **Single-coarsening nested dissection (`BuildEtree` / `GetSeparator`):** Build the patch quotient graph **once** (no repeated multilevel coarsening per level), update node/edge weights incrementally as separators are removed (§5), bipartition the small quotient graph (via METIS in the paper), lift the boundary to a vertex-separator superset, and refine it (via METIS node refinement). Recurse `nd_level` (9–10) levels while constructing the binary elimination tree (`etree`).
4. **Local ordering & concatenation:** Run AMD independently on each tree node's induced subgraph, then concatenate in post-order (CPU cache locality) or level-order (GPU wavefront).

### 2.2 Published numbers and trade-offs

- **Baseline:** Replaces **METIS** (not AMD) inside NVIDIA cuDSS and Intel MKL.
- **Speedups:** 4.58× geometric-mean ordering speedup (up to 10.27× vs METIS, 2.27× vs ParMETIS); end-to-end geometric mean **3.51× on GPU cuDSS** (up to 6.62×) and **1.76× on CPU Intel MKL** (up to 2.55×).
- **Size threshold (§6.1, §6.6):** Beneficial only above **~50K vertices on cuDSS** and **~100K vertices on Intel MKL**; less competitive for `n < 10^5`.
- **Fill-in trade-off (§6.6):** Patch-based separators increase `nnz(L)/nnz(A)` by **+15.44%** (small meshes) and **+6.07%** (large meshes) vs METIS separators; local AMD adds **+6.67% to +10.78%** fill vs local METIS.
- **Volumetric meshes (§7):** Preliminary tet-mesh test slowed down to **0.83×** when patching with `METIS_KWay`, showing volumetric inputs require a fast dedicated patcher.

### 2.3 Fit against `geometry3Sharp`'s `SparseCholesky` / `CholeskyAnalysis`

Measured repo baselines ([SparseCholeskyBenchmark/README.md](../../benchmarks/SparseCholeskyBenchmark/README.md), [BiharmonicDeformationBenchmark/README.md](../../benchmarks/BiharmonicDeformationBenchmark/README.md)):
- `CholeskyAnalysis` currently uses **AMD (`MinimumDegree`)**, not METIS. On 55K–61K `CrossField` meshes (`h=0.017`, 4 cores), AMD ordering takes **0.22–0.26 s** (~15–25% of the 0.83–1.96 s total); the 2 s / 50K gate is already met.
- The real solver bottleneck on 50K–100K+ meshes is **numeric factorization and solves**, driven by AMD's unbalanced elimination tree (root supernode width 961–1764 on a 55K sphere, 36–56% of numeric time, 68% critical path). `BiharmonicMeshDeformer` on 100K vertices takes 4.55 s warm / 6.96 s cold (2-ring stencil, denser factor).
- `SparseCholeskyBenchmark/README.md` already measured the upper bound for **nested dissection** on 55K–61K `CrossField`: **1.8× fewer flops, critical path 49% instead of 68%** (changes factor and result bits → owner decision).

### 2.4 Verdict

- **Adopt the paper's implementation as-is: No.** It targets METIS-dominated GPU/MKL pipelines at 100K–1.8M vertices and depends on GPU patching + METIS.
- **Adopt the single-quotient-graph ND architecture in pure C#: Conditionally yes, for 100K+ systems.** Replacing METIS with a small deterministic CPU greedy patcher on `CholeskyAnalysis`'s block graph (e.g., 100K nodes / patch size 128 → ~780 quotient nodes), simple deterministic bipartitioning + FM vertex-separator refinement on that tiny quotient graph, and **parallel local `MinimumDegree`** on the independent tree nodes would avoid implementing full multilevel METIS while delivering lower fill-in, a shorter parallel critical path (~49% vs ~68%), and parallel ordering.
- **Prerequisites before any build:**
  1. Owner decision on bit change (opt-in ordering mode vs default switch, since changing `Perm` changes `L` and golden solution hashes).
  2. Benchmark-only upper-bound spike comparing patch-guided ND vs current AMD on `SparseCholeskyBenchmark` (40K–120K 2D, 27K 3D), `CrossFieldBenchmark` (55K–61K), and `BiharmonicDeformationBenchmark` (100K) to verify that the +6–15% patch-ND fill penalty vs METIS still clearly beats our current AMD.

## 3. Audit: Can We Use *Fast and Exact Winding Numbers for Triangle Meshes* (FEWN) Further?

### 3.1 Relationship between FEWN and Antipodal (and why Antipodal's formulation stays our base)

As established in [LEVY_CLASSIFICATION_WINDING2026.md](../design/LEVY_CLASSIFICATION_WINDING2026.md), **FEWN** (Xie, Hafner, Wojtan, ACM TOG 45(4), Art. 41, 2026) and **The Antipodal Method** (Martens, Trettner, Bessmeltsev, ACM TOG 45(4), Art. 43, 2026) compute the exact same decomposition:

$$w_M(p) = \chi(p, d) + \frac{1}{4\pi} \sum_{(a,b) \in \partial M} \Omega(a - p, b - p, \text{apex})$$

where $\chi(p, d)$ is the signed ray-crossing count along ray $d$ and the sum runs over exterior edges $\partial M$ (edges with nonzero net oriented count). FEWN places the closing-cone apex at a finite point behind $p$ along $-d$; Antipodal takes the limit at infinity along $-d$ (yielding a simpler unnormalized `atan2` formula).

Three practical reasons keep **Antipodal's formulation** strictly preferable to FEWN's implementation details inside `geometry3Sharp`:
1. **Degenerate rays:** FEWN rejects exact predicates + SoS because Embree runs in float, substituting unspecified near-edge/near-surface thresholds and local closed-patch solid-angle evaluation. `geometry3Sharp` already has exact `Orient3d` + SoS coupling (no ad-hoc thresholds, consistent one-sided convention on the surface).
2. **Performance:** Antipodal's authors report 7.2× faster queries and 1.5× faster preprocessing than FEWN on 1,789 open Thingi10K meshes (16³ grid, single thread).
3. **Code license:** FEWN's repository (`BoundaryBlaze`) has **no license** (all rights reserved — must never be read or ported; paper is CC BY 4.0 only), whereas Antipodal's code is MIT (paper CC BY 4.0).

Therefore, the real engineering question is: **can we extend our ray-plus-boundary exact GWN (Antipodal/FEWN decomposition) beyond `ExactMeshBoolean`, where it is currently private?**

### 3.2 Complete audit of winding-number call sites in `geometry3Sharp`

### 3.3 Concrete takeaway & recommended order
