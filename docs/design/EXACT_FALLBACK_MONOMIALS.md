# Exact predicates on doubles of any exponent: the monomial accumulator

The exact stage of `ExactPredicates.Orient2d`, `Orient3d` and `InCircle` for coordinates outside the range of the
expansions, [2^-100, 2^100]. It replaces the BigInteger determinants (`orient2d_bigint`, `orient3d_bigint`,
`incircle_bigint`) there; they stay in `ExactPredicates` as the reference of the tests and `InSphere` keeps its
BigInteger stage. Owner decision of 2026-11-12 after the study of the exact number representation
([roadmap](../roadmap/exact-boolean.md), issue #711; the study's rows are on branch `runs/exact-numbers`).

## 1. Why

`ExactPredicates` decides a sign with a floating-point filter, then with the 62-bit integer stage (`Orient3d` only),
then with Shewchuk's adaptive expansions if every coordinate is 0 or in [2^-100, 2^100], and otherwise with all
coordinates as BigInteger on a common power-of-two scale. That last stage is rare on ordinary inputs (0 calls in 131
operations on Thingi10K models), but it is the whole cost of inputs whose coordinates span many binary orders inside
one predicate. The fuzz units of issue #711 (two shapes of 8 to 16 vertices, corners near 1e-284 next to coordinates
near 1e-32, a span of 2^840) spent 81 % of 298 s in it: an aligned coordinate has about 900 bits, the determinant 2700,
and one `Orient3d` took 5 us and allocated 4.6 KB.

Rescaling does not help: the span is inside one predicate, so no common power of two brings the coordinates into the
range of the expansions (the boolean study measured 3 % of the calls admitted). A dense integer on the stack is the
same algorithm without the heap and gains 2.5x. The coordinates are sparse, though: 53 significant bits each.

## 2. The method

Every finite double is `m 2^e` with an integer `|m| <= 2^53 - 1` and `e` in [-1074, 971]. Each predicate is the
determinant of the points with a column of ones, which needs no differences of coordinates:

- `Orient2d`: `det [a 1; b 1; c 1]`, 6 monomials of degree 2;
- `Orient3d`: `det [a 1; b 1; c 1; d 1]` up to the sign of the library's convention, 24 monomials of degree 3;
- `InCircle`: `det [p.x p.y |p|^2 1]` over a, b, c, d, 48 monomials of degree 4.

A monomial is a product of `d` mantissas, an integer below `2^(53 d)` (`d` limbs of 64 bits), times 2 to the sum of
its `d` exponents. With `emin` the smallest exponent of a nonzero coordinate of the call, every monomial is an integer
multiple of `2^(d emin)`. The kernel adds the positive monomials, each shifted left by (the sum of its exponents) -
`d emin` bits, into one magnitude accumulator and the negative ones into a second, and returns the comparison of the
two from the top limb. A monomial with a zero factor is skipped.

There is no floating-point operation in the kernel, so there is no rounding, no overflow or underflow, no error bound
and no FMA question; NaN and infinity throw `ArgumentException`, as before. The accumulators are `stackalloc`'ed and
cleared per call; nothing is allocated on the heap.

## 3. Bounds

With `s = emax - emin` over the nonzero coordinates of a call, `T` monomials and `g = ceil(log2 T)`, each accumulator
is below `T (2^53 - 1)^d 2^(d s) < 2^(d s + 53 d + g)`, so

    L(s) = floor(d s / 64) + ceil((53 d + g + 63) / 64)

limbs hold it (the `+ 63` is the shift of the lowest monomial inside its first limb). A monomial is written to `d + 1`
limbs starting at limb `floor(offset / 64)` with `offset <= d s`; the pass checks that this is inside `L(s)`. The
carry of an addition therefore ends inside the accumulator. The largest span of two finite doubles is 2045.

The table is generated (`tools/PredicateCompiler --ddoc`); `GeneratedDoublePredicatesTests` keeps it equal to the
derivation and checks the rule with exact integers for every `s` from 0 to 2045:

| predicate | degree d | monomials T | guard bits g | bits of a monomial | accumulator limbs L(s), s = emax - emin | limbs at s = 0 | limbs at s = 2045 | stack bytes at s = 2045 (both accumulators) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Orient2d` | 2 | 6 | 3 | 106 | `floor(2 s / 64) + 3` | 3 | 66 | 1056 |

## 4. The generator

`tools/PredicateCompiler/Monomials.cs` (the monomial pass) reads `dpredicate Name(points) inputs xy|xyz` entries
(`tools/PredicateCompiler/doubles.spec`): the formula as for `ipredicate`, with the double coordinates as inputs. It
expands the sign expression into monomials (like terms merged), requires one degree and coefficients of +1 or -1,
derives the table above and writes `math/Generated/DoublePredicates.cs`: one helper `add<d>` per degree (the product of
`d` mantissas factor by factor with `FixedArith.MulHigh`, the shift, the addition with carry) and one kernel per
predicate with a line per monomial. The file is checked in, is exact code for the analyzers (`.editorconfig`:
`math/Generated/*.cs`) and is kept equal to the generator's output by `GeneratedFile_IsUpToDate`.

The helpers write through pointers into a stack buffer, so the bound of section 3 is also checked at run time: a
monomial whose `d + 1` limbs, or a carry that, would leave the accumulator throws `InvalidOperationException` before
anything is written outside it (one compare per monomial; an offset below the accumulator is caught by the same
unsigned compare). The rule excludes it; the check turns an error of the rule, of the generator or of a caller into
an exception instead of a write outside the buffer (`AddHelpers_ThrowOutsideTheAccumulator`).

    dotnet run --project tools/PredicateCompiler -- --dcode tools/PredicateCompiler/doubles.spec math/Generated/DoublePredicates.cs
    dotnet run --project tools/PredicateCompiler -- --ddoc tools/PredicateCompiler/doubles.spec docs/design/EXACT_FALLBACK_MONOMIALS.md

`InSphere` is not generated: its 360 monomials of degree 5 are slower than BigInteger inside the range of the
expansions (10 us against 3 us), and its exact stage does not run in the alpha wrap, its only user (0 fallbacks counted).

## 6. Sources

Written from the method, no code was ported. The accumulator is Kulisch's long accumulator for exact dot products
(Kulisch and Snyder 2011) extended to products of two to four factors; the reduction of a determinant to the exact
sign of a sum of products is that of Ratschek and Rokne (ESSA, 1999) and Gavrilova, Ratschek and Rokne (2000); the
generated straight-line integer code per predicate follows Fortune and Van Wyk (1996); Mascarenhas (2021) treats the
same problem with scaled doubles. See `docs/SOURCES.md`.
