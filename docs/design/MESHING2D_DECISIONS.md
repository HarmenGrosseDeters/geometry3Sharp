# 2D meshing: the domain decisions, questioned

Status: proposal for the owner, written 2026-10-31 by a session that implemented nothing. It questions the decisions in the parked documents of PR #201
(`docs/design/MESHING2D_M2.md` and `MESHING2D_M2_API.md`, branch `docs/meshing2d-m2-design`) and fixes the defaults that the implementing session builds until the
owner says otherwise.

**The approvals.** #201 records owner approvals dated 2026-10-30 for its domain decisions and five API groups, and calls the API frozen. The owner disputes this
(note at the top of both documents), and nothing outside those documents supports it. This table therefore counts only
[MESHING2D_SPEC.md](MESHING2D_SPEC.md), section 5, as a decision of the owner. "Recorded" below means: #201 says it was approved, and that is all we know.

**Owner decision, 2026-11-01: D5 changed to role-based nesting (islands in lakes are meshed), see its row; Q7 clarified accordingly.**

**Owner decision, 2026-11-01: D14 as proposed by the literature check: `m2.snap` is built as iterated snap rounding (Halperin and Packer 2002) at
the explicit tolerance, as its own PR after M2 and M3 are merged.**

**Owner decision, 2026-11-01 (later): D14 made precise after the look-before-you-build check (`MESHING2D_SNAP.md`, #272), as recommended:**
1. the bounded-drift variant of iterated snap rounding (ISRBD, Packer 2008), so that O11's displacement bound holds;
2. hot regions of radius tol centred at the vertices (O11's wording), not grid pixels;
3. vertices that snap to nothing keep their input coordinates (no output grid);
4. the gap band is documented: merged below tol, kept above 2 tol, either in between (O11's "relative bound").

Semantics aligned with SafeRounding (`SAFE_ROUNDING.md`): drift reported, a strict mode with a failure status, merge below the tolerance.

**Owner decision, 2026-11-01 (final, supersedes points 1 and 2 above):** after the full texts were read (`MESHING2D_SNAP.md`, section
3b; ISRBD collapses at small angles, vertex-centred hot regions void SR's proofs, Hershberger 2025):
1. **snap rounding on a grid plus the restore step of SRR** (Belussi, Migliorini, Negri, Pelagatti 2016), with SR's contact rules
   unchanged;
2. **pixel width w = tol.** O11's guarantee becomes: merged below tol/2, never merged above 2 tol (L∞), the grid decides in between
   (the gap band). Data on the tolerance grid stays put; off-grid data moves by at most tol/2 per axis.

Points 3 and 4 stand in this form. Band cases (F-15 at 0.91 tol, F-18/a at tol/2) accept merged or unmerged.

**Owner decision, 2026-11-01: all output vertices on the grid**, Steiner and split points included; a product requirement, not a later
option. The design is proposed in `MESHING2D_SNAP.md` section 3g and awaits the coordinator:
- rounding at insertion, with no final pass and no relocation;
- refused grid points make a triangle grid-blocked;
- unsplittable subsegments stay judged;
- an angle floor of two pixels, measured.

**Owner decisions, 2026-10-31: D1, D5 and D9 as recommended below.** So: output in doubles, with validity decided on those doubles; a hole removes everything inside it, with a diagnostic when an outer ring lies inside a hole, and islands are not part of this; longest edge at most h at the centroid. The others have a default that is fine unless the owner objects. Each default is cheap to change: the "where it
lives" column names the one place in the oracle, so that a different decision costs an edit there, a changed fixture and golden rows with a stated reason.

## Literature check of D1 to D19 (2026-11-01)

Owner's request: these are fundamental questions with published answers, so check each decision against them. Papers, standards and
documentation only (no GPL code, nothing of Triangle or Triangle.NET). Full references in [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md), section 2D meshing.

| # | Decision | Standard or paper | Verdict | What to change if it differs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Output in doubles, valid on those doubles; exact arithmetic inside | Exact geometric computation: decisions exact, constructions rounded separately (Yap 1997); exact predicates on floating-point input (Shewchuk 1997); rounding an arrangement to a representable set while keeping its topology (Greene and Yao 1986, Milenkovic 1988) | Agrees: exact decisions on the doubles the caller receives; crossings rounded, the mesh re-established on the rounded points | None. ## Findings beyond the table

- **#201's tests never existed in the repository.** Its acceptance draft and spike live in a session directory on one machine (`C:\Users\Harmen\.copilot\...`). None of its numbers can be re-run from the repository. Its
  "independent" reviews failed with HTTP 502, as it says itself.
- **"Frozen" was premature in any case:** its section 8 lists the routing as unproven and M1 as not accepted, on the same page.
- **What is good in #201 and worth keeping:** the observation that a rounded crossing must not be fed back into exact predicates as if exact; the F-case mapping per increment; the mutation list; the correct remark that displacement
  of a piece is bounded by that of its ends.
